How I Would Help Build Europe for Ben

Ben | Pascal Schumachers | 5 markets, 10 dimensions

Ben is scaling, and the global benefits buyer does not sit in one country. That buyer sits in a headquarters, running benefits for staff across dozens of markets at once, and I am built to reach them wherever they are.

I sell from the UK, Ben's core market, and I operate three of Europe's richest buyer pools in the buyer's own language: the UK from my base, DACH as a German dual national, and Benelux in fluent Dutch. But the reach is not the point on its own. The point is what I close with it. That buyer often sits in the US, and I have closed global programmes run from US HR organisations as readily as from European headquarters. My biggest deals have been exactly that: one buying committee, many countries, one HQ. That is the motion Ben wins on.

What follows is how I would build. I land new logos through the channel and my own network where it converts fastest, then over 18 months turn that into a repeatable playbook the team can build on. I have joined and built inside scaling companies for 25 years, always turning an early stage presence into a mature business that sells on value. I would start by closing deals and grow toward leading the region that follows.

The analysis below scores five markets across ten dimensions that reflect how Ben actually wins, with my network of more than 4,000 senior enterprise contacts mapped to each one.

Building inside a scaling company is not a risk in my profile, it is the pattern of my career
I have joined companies at exactly the stage Ben is at now, early and scaling, and turned an unproven presence into a mature, business value led operation. It is the environment where I do my best work, and the reason I am drawn to Ben.
EWB (later National Instruments). Joined at around 80 employees globally. First on the ground in Germany and closed the biggest deal in the history of the company with the federal education market, built the territory from a standing start. The company was later acquired by National Instruments.
Gladius, The Gambia. Sent as CEO to launch the country operation from nothing. Pivoted the entire business model from hardware to turnkey delivery, scaled to around 50 people, and won the largest government contract in the company's history: immigration offices, police HQ, and central bank infrastructure.
VMware AirWatch. Joined at around 240 people globally as employee 169. Rode the hypergrowth and Dell EMC era as the organisation scaled past 2,000.
Phenom. Employee seven in Europe and first on the ground in DACH. Built the region from zero into one of the strongest in the company, selling AI native technology against entrenched legacy platforms.
An early, scaling company is where I do my best work. I bring the playbook for turning that stage into a repeatable, mature revenue engine, because I have built it four times.
